# Common pitfalls (cn)

> This is a translation of the cn market package with cultural notes; it is not a separate market package. Where it differs from the Chinese file, the Chinese file is authoritative.
> Each entry: pattern name / how to spot it / typical trigger / skeleton. Only the pattern is extracted; no specific cases are included.
> The order is the harm ranking in the author's sample. Module A-5 takes the top 3 when there is no personal handbook; Module B-4 uses only the pattern names in this file, and new findings go to "pending observations".
> Delivery-type flaws (filler-phrase density, speaking speed) are not in this library: they are counted by Module B-5, and enter neither the pitfalls nor the outcome prediction.

---

## 1. Late ammunition

- **How to spot it**: your strongest mechanism-level evidence does not appear in the self-intro or the first 10 minutes; it is left to the close or never said at all. The first sentence of the self-intro contains a downgrading word of the "support / collaborate / coordinate / help" kind.
- **Typical trigger**: the self-introduction; "start by introducing yourself"; the interviewer opens by pressing on a specific question, so the self-intro is skipped.
- **Skeleton**: D (portable one-liner: one piece of mechanism-level evidence with a number within the first minute); the A-7 self-intro structure.

## 2. No prebuilt attribution

- **How to spot it**: when asked "which part of this growth is attributed to what", you break it down on the spot, cannot produce layers, or brush past with "the overall result was good".
- **Typical trigger**: Type B pressing on stated figures and wording; any "from X to Y" number on the résumé being followed up.
- **Skeleton**: B (time-sequenced split / funnel location / honest fallback).

## 3. Misused clarifying questions

- **How to spot it**: asking a clarifying question even for a single-sentence data question, dragging the pace; or answering a multi-layer question without enumerating first, and dropping a layer.
- **Typical trigger**: Type B short, fast pressing on numbers; Type F multi-layer abstract questions.
- **Skeleton**: no dedicated skeleton. Rule: answer single-sentence data questions directly; for multi-layer questions, enumerate layer by layer before answering (the first step of Skeleton F).

## 4. Self-downgrading

- **How to spot it**: under pressure, volunteering flaws of the "delivery / structure / data" kind; or talking the role down ("responsible for supporting X").
- **Typical trigger**: the biggest-weakness question; failing to follow on after a follow-up; the first sentence of the self-intro.
- **Skeleton**: C (controlled concession: concede only an industry-language gap + a time limit + a transferable method).

## 5. Opinion instead of method

- **How to spot it**: when asked "how would you evaluate / how would you falsify / how do you judge", you give a conclusion without an evaluation design (metrics, thresholds, falsification condition).
- **Typical trigger**: Type F; any "what do you think of X".
- **Skeleton**: F (framework + evaluation + falsification); A (reversal condition).

## 6. Runaway case

- **How to spot it**: a single case exceeds 90 seconds; the total answer to a case question exceeds 3 minutes; you keep talking until the interviewer interrupts or changes the question.
- **Typical trigger**: a Type A-2 case question; "tell me about the project you are proudest of".
- **Skeleton**: H (each route ≤ 90 seconds, close within 3 minutes; if you run over, stop and ask "would you like me to expand on this direction").

## 7. Wrong retrieval order

- **How to spot it**: before answering a case question you asked about the user profile but not about the current state of the business and the no-go areas; the plan lands on channels they do not use or cannot use.
- **Typical trigger**: Type A-2 "if it were you, how would you do it"; heavily regulated industries.
- **Skeleton**: H (three questions in order: current state → no-go areas → profile).

## 8. Missed implicit question

- **How to spot it**: "do you use / buy / play it yourself" is answered as a factual question — you describe your own usage habits without picking up the layer "are you the target user, and if not, how do you understand users".
- **Typical trigger**: the middle-to-late part of a Type A-2 interview; any consumer-facing product role.
- **Skeleton**: J (implicit user question).

## 9. Contradictory direction answer

- **How to spot it**: the interviewer offers a step up ("your experience in this area is very scarce"), and the candidate answers "that's my comfort zone, I want a new challenge", pushing the step away; the stated direction is the opposite of your own capability band.
- **Typical trigger**: Type G "where do you want to go"; any redirect scenario.
- **Skeleton**: I (direction question).

## 10. Misheard question

- **How to spot it**: a question built on an abstract noun (regulation, environment, latitude, mechanism) is answered off-target the first time, and the interviewer asks it again.
- **Typical trigger**: Types C / F; questions about industry constraints.
- **Skeleton**: no dedicated skeleton. Rule: first restate in one line "are you asking about X or about Y", then answer (the enumerate-and-restate first step of Skeleton F).

## 11. Credit given away

- **How to spot it**: when describing results you volunteer "I wasn't responsible for the later part" or "this was mainly done by others", giving away cumulative numbers that could be attributed to you; or only one of the several numbers in your consistency checklist comes out.
- **Typical trigger**: Type G reconstructing your experience stage by stage and asking about the boundary of your authority; any "did you do this yourself".
- **Skeleton**: D (portable one-liner; bring out the related numbers from the consistency checklist together). This does not conflict with "answer the boundary of authority truthfully": authority truthfully, credit not over-conceded.

## Cultural notes

This taxonomy was written from experienced-hire interviews in the Chinese internet industry. The following items may not hold in other markets.

- "Late ammunition" and the self-intro rules assume a 3-minute self-introduction in the first business round. In many markets the expected self-intro is 60–90 seconds, so the "first minute" thresholds shift.
